How We Catered 2,000 Guests at Taj Falaknuma Palace

Chef Tariq Siddiqui 29 September 2026 6 Min Read 22 Comments

Perched 2,000 feet above Hyderabad, Taj Falaknuma Palace - the 'Mirror of the Sky' - once hosted kings, tsars, and British viceroys. In November last year, Zaffran was commissioned to cater an international destination wedding for 2,000 dignitaries and royalty.

Preparing 2,000 plates of commercial rice in modern hotel steam kettles is straightforward. But preparing 2,000 portions of authentic, firewood-simmered, dough-sealed dum biryani without sacrificing a single degree of tradition is an operational miracle.

1. The 30-Meter Charcoal Trenches

Palace heritage protocols strictly forbade modern gas burners on the palace gardens. Our engineering team constructed custom insulated steel trenches lined with firebricks on the lower terrace, fueled entirely by cured babool charcoal.

Temperature probes monitored the heat beneath each quadrant of the trench. To achieve the delicate balance of dum pukht, 28 culinary assistants rotated hot coals onto the lids of 22 colossal 80kg copper degs simultaneously.

How We Catered 2,000 Guests at Taj Falaknuma Palace

2. The 22 Sealed Copper Degs

Each copper deg contained 35kg of tender baby mutton, 30kg of two-year aged Himalayan basmati, 8kg of pure desi ghee, and 45g of pure Pampore saffron. That represents over 750kg of meat and 660kg of aged rice in continuous harmony.

The rim of each deg was hermetically sealed with 6kg of fresh whole-wheat atta dough. For four hours, the terraces of Falaknuma filled with the sweet, intoxicating scent of slow-rendering marrow and saffron steam.

Chef Tariq's Banquet Rule

"When catering for thousands, never cut the dough seal in the kitchen and transfer the rice into chafing dishes. Bring the blazing hot handis directly to the buffet tables and unseal them in front of the guests."

3. The Synchronized Unsealing Ceremony

At precisely 9:00 PM, as the royal shehnai signaled the opening of the banquet, 22 ustads raised ceremonial brass knives and sliced the dough rims in unison. A unified cloud of steam rose into the night sky, drawing applause from guests who rushed to the counters.

22 Copper Degs

Specially hammered 80kg capacity brass degs crafted by Old City tinsmiths.

750kg Heritage Mutton

Pre-marinated in cold-pressed mustard oil, raw papaya, and royal potli masala.

120 Kitchen Crew

Dedicated team of ustads, coal tenders, and silver-service servers.

0g Compromise

Every grain fluffy, separate, and basted in golden bone marrow essence.

4. The Royal Guest Verdict

By midnight, every single deg had been cleaned to the shining bottom copper. Critics and diplomats from six continents proclaimed it the finest biryani they had ever experienced - proof that true royal craftsmanship scales to any height when tradition is never compromised.
