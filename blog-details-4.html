The Host's Guide: Calculating Biryani Quantities for 50+ Guests

Chef Imran Khan 11 October 2026 7 Min Read 18 Comments

Hosting a grand gathering - whether a wedding reception, anniversary, Eid feast, or corporate town hall - comes with immense pressure. The single greatest fear of every Indian host is running out of biryani before the last guest has taken their plate.

Yet ordering wildly in excess leads to painful food wastage and degraded flavor. Having catered over 1,400 weddings and corporate galas, Zaffran operates on an exact mathematical formula that guarantees every guest enjoys royal generosity.

1. The Golden 400g Per-Guest Formula

When ordering biryani, hosts often ask for 'number of plates' without realizing commercial portion sizes vary wildly. In royal dum cooking, the gold standard for an adult guest is 380g to 420g of cooked biryani (including 2 succulent pieces of meat totaling 180g-200g).

For a gathering with 50 adult guests, you require precisely 20kg of finished, steaming biryani. If your guest list includes a mix of adults and children under 12, calculate children at 220g (roughly 0.6 of an adult portion).

2. Sizing Your Degs: 5kg, 10kg, or 15kg?

Never order one massive 40kg cauldron for a party if it cannot be served all at once. Once the dough seal of a deg is broken, the internal steam escapes, and the remaining rice begins cooling and drying out within 25 minutes.

Instead, order in modular degs: two 10kg degs or four 5kg handis. Keep the second deg sealed over glowing charcoal embers while your guests enjoy the first. When the first handi empties, unseal the second fresh and piping hot.

Chef Imran's Veg/Non-Veg Rule

"In a typical Indian crowd of 100 guests where both are served, 70% of guests will take non-veg mutton/chicken and 30% veg. But always prepare 40% vegetarian, because non-vegetarians will frequently sample the Paneer Tikka Biryani too!"

3. The Accompaniment Ratio: Salan & Raita

A royal meal requires balanced sides. For every 10 guests, plan 750ml of Mirchi Ka Salan and 800ml of Burani Raita. Never skimp on fresh mint-coriander salad and quartered lemons.

10kg Deg (25-30 Pax)

Includes 5kg raw basmati + 6kg marinated meat + 2L salan + 2L raita.

15kg Deg (35-40 Pax)

Includes 7.5kg raw basmati + 9kg marinated meat + full accompaniment buffet.

Charcoal Warmers

Keeps sealed degs at a consistent 68°C without drying or burning the base.

Insulated Handis

Earthen pots that retain thermal core heat for up to 3.5 hours in transit.

4. Staging and Timing Your Buffet

Schedule your handi delivery 30 to 45 minutes before dinner is called. Our catering technicians set up low-heat charcoal braziers beneath each copper handi, ensuring that whether guests eat at 8:30 PM or 10:30 PM, the rice is aromatic and sizzling.
